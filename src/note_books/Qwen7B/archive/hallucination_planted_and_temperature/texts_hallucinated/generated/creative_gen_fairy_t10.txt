In a small village near the edge of the dense forest, there lived a miller and his son, Roger. Roger, the only child, was known for his brave heart and their mill for his honest deeds. One day, a royal messenger arrived at their door, bearing a message from the king himself. The messenger informed them that the king had decided to marry someone from the village, but the suitors would not be chosen by a simple test of wealth or nobility, but by the king's impossible tasks. The first suitor to accomplish all three tasks would earn the privilege of winning the princess’s hand in marriage.

Roger, hearing the news, could not help but feel a mixture of excitement and nervousness. To his surprise, the king’s decree was announced to the entire village. The tasks were:
1. To grind a full year’s supply of flour in a single day.
2. To subdue a wild boar without the use of a weapon.
3. To avert the king’s immersion in a chaotic dance of a hundred men and emerge victorious.

Roger, however, was not one to shy away from challenges. He promised to ask his father for advice before setting off, but his father had no advice to offer instead of his own encouragement. The miller simply advised, “Son, it’s up to you to find your own path, but remember, you are brave and strong. Don’t give up.”

The first task was the most daunting. Roger had to grind a full year’s supply of flour in one day, which would have overwhelmed anyone but him. The mill was small, with a single millstone. It took him the entire day, but Roger, through ingenuity and sheer will, used the daylight hours to grind as much flour as possible. The noise of the grinding persisted well into the night, attracting the attention of the townspeople who gathered to witness the spectacle. Legend had it that even the earth shook under the strong grinding, and the room was filled with flour dust that created a delightful aroma.

Next, was the task of subduing a wild boar. Roger had no weapon, so he used his wit and agility to distract the boar and then calm it. He remembered a nearby pond where wild boars were known to gather. Roger set a simple trap with a carefully placed branch on the ground. When the boar came to drink, it triggered the branch, which landed on its head, effectively injuring the boar but not killing it. Roger tended to the wounded animal and brought it to the king, who was impressed by Roger’s bravery and cleverness.

The final task was the most challenging one. The king introduced Roger to a parading ground where a hundred young men were going to engage in a chaotic dance. The king himself was to be part of the parade. The task was to maneuver through the chaos and emerge victorious, meaning to be the first to reach the king and knock him off his feet with a bow. Roger, however, did not participate in such dances, and he had no idea how to navigate through such a ruckus. But as the dance began, he saw the key to his success. As the chaos grew, Roger appeared, dressed in a simple farm outfit with a large full moon on his tunic—it was a full moon night.

The boisterous men ignored him, thinking he was just a comical figure riding a bucking horse. Roger used this distraction to his advantage. He moved slowly and deliberately, weaving through the crowd with the agility of a cat. When he reached the king, he explained that the moon predicted a new beginning, and a moon’s bow—known as a solar eclipse in the old tales—would signify the right to royalty. The king chuckled and handed him his victory bow.

The next morning, all the villagers arrived to the castle to witness the wedding ceremony. Miraculously, just as the first shoots of dawn broke through the horizon, a full eclipse happened. Roger, with the bow of the King, knocked the king off his feet. The crowd gasped, thinking it was magic, but Roger knew it was just a stroke of luck. The king, delighted by Roger’s ingenuity and bravery, proclaimed him a true heir to the throne and the best man for his daughter.

From that day, Roger and the princess were known as the king and queen of a festival where moon and sun were celebrated, bringing joy and harmony to the land. And the villagers, once they knew the story of the humble miller’s son, gathered every year at the behest of the new queen for the festival of the new year, a celebration of hope, knowledge, and a new beginning.
