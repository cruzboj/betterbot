Detective Eleanor Marlowe stood in front of the hotel’s front desk, her eyes scanning the room service menu. The hallway was quiet, save for the distant hum of the air conditioning. She wore a deep blue suit, the fabric smooth under her fingers as she smoothed it. The hotel, a grand Victorian structure, was emptying of guests for the night, and the staff was winding down for the evening. Tonight, she was here for a single purpose: to solve a theft.

The manager, a man named Thomas, approached her with a business card in hand. “Miss Marlowe, this is the reservation for the stolen item,” he said, his voice a mix of disbelief and concern. “We have a client who was robbed last night.”

“Thank you, Mr. Thomas. Can you give me the details?” Eleanor asked, her curiosity piqued. She had been called in for a case, and the hotel, with its rich history and opulent decor, provided a perfect setting for her to begin.

“Of course,” Thomas said, handing over a folder. “The room was reported empty in the morning, but a necklace was missing. It belonged to a guest, Mrs. Eliza Greene, who checked out earlier than expected.”

Eleanor rifled through the folder, her eyes scanning the contents. “What was the necklace made of? Do we have a description?”

“Mrs. Greene described it as an antique, diamond and emerald necklace,” Thomas replied. “It was a family heirloom, quite valuable. I’m afraid it was not replaced before her departure.”

Eleanor nodded, her mind already working through the possibilities. “Any guests who checked out late last night? Anything unusual?”

Thomas consulted his records. “A guest named William Harper, a businessman, checked out late last night. He was seen in the bar around midnight, and he was the last person to enter the room. However, he was under the influence of alcohol and claims he did not enter Mrs. Greene’s room.”

The detective’s gaze sharpened. “Did you check the room for signs of forced entry?”

“Yes, there were no signs of forced entry,” Thomas answered. “But it’s a possibility. We also have a housekeeper who was supposed to clean the room this morning but didn’t.”

“Interesting,” Eleanor murmured, her thoughts turning. “Bring me the housekeeper. I need to talk to her.”

The housekeeper, a woman named Maria, was a petite woman with a nervous demeanor. She wrung her hands as Eleanor questioned her.

“Did you enter Mrs. Greene’s room today?” Eleanor asked, her voice calm but firm.

“Yes, but only to clean it,” Maria said, her voice shaking. “I didn’t see anyone else in there. I followed the usual protocol and didn’t find anything unusual.”

Eleanor’s eyes flicked to the housekeeping log. “Did you record your activities as usual?”

“Yes, of course,” Maria said, reaching for a clipboard. “Here, it’s all recorded.”

Eleanor studied the log, her eye catching on something small. “Wait, there’s a discrepancy in your log. You checked the room at 8 AM but didn’t record the cleaning until 10 AM.”

Maria’s face paled. “I… I didn’t mean to. I was running late that day and forgot to record it.”

“Interesting,” Eleanor said, her voice neutral. “And have you ever seen William Harper in the hotel before?”

“Yes, I have,” Maria said, her voice almost a whisper. “He was often here. He always seemed to be in a hurry, like he had somewhere to be.”

Eleanor leaned back, her mind racing. “I think we need to talk to William Harper again. There’s something about this case that doesn’t add up.”

As she left the hotel, Eleanor’s thoughts were a whirlwind. The housekeeper’s mistake, the strange behavior of William Harper, and the lack of forced entry all pointed to a possible solution. She walked back to her car, her mind already forming a plan.

“Mr. Harper,” Eleanor said, her tone steady. “I need to talk to you about a recent theft at the hotel.”

Harper’s eyes narrowed. “What theft? I don’t know anything about this.”

“Mrs. Greene’s necklace was stolen,” Eleanor said, spreading the folder on his desk. “And the housekeeper claimed she forgot to record the cleaning of the room.”
