Evelyn had always known that magic was about to ambush her at Cornish Walden, a school for the young and truly gifted. As the first-year student studying in a larger, more ancient hall than the Harry Potter library could ever illustrate, she along with the other sixty-nine freshmen was whisked away from the mundane and thrown into an unstructured, yet demanding curriculum that outlined her seemingly endless possibilities. 

The nights at Cornish Walden seemed to stretch out without end as a select few peered into the perimeters around them in hopes of glimpsing a fleeting magic that was too invisible. Restless nights and broken sleep were a norm for Evelyn, but she had never felt more alive. 

October 30th was a hallmark day at Cornish Walden as the school’s famed “All Horse Elections” was held. Students who aspired to lead their houses were placed in separate rooms where they exercised their magical prowess through various tasks and challenges. The one with the highest-formed will and the most compelling display of skill would be elected and dubbed the house’s horse. It was an extraordinary and exclusive honor.

January 20 marked the final “All Horse Elections.” The day before, the school’s annual “Maldi Exams” were scheduled to begin. Some students were assigned these tests to help them along their magical journey. No one was considered exempt, so for Evelyn, the stress was mounting. She would have to get her grade up and maintain it between now and next year. The Ministry of Magic in London was counting on her skill to produce future wizards, and her family would no doubt look upon her lavishly if she excelled. 

Evelyn’s nerves were frayed. She sat up a little later than most students, her eyes straining in the half-light of her home room. All throughout the school, students were huddled over scrolls and focusing on various bits of magic. At wooden desks that felt like they were often off-balance due to the peculiar nature of the room they were floating in, Evelyn was reviewing her notes from a pre-exam spellcasting review class. She needed a wand. There was absolutely no way she was going to perform the required incantations and management of magic without a loaded magical mahogany and her list of cores. Evelyn knew just where to go for help, or at the very least, a place she thought no one would look.

Her midnight path led her to the open chambers of the school’s library. It was still and almost pitch black, the only light coming from buzzing spell crystals that traveled around the vast and sparkling white walls in an automated fashion. She wasn’t alone in seeking help. Students were huddled behind spell scrolls or sketchbooks; no one was cutting. They all knew that the library would be sorted immediately to make sure every spell book and ingredient was at the proper desk. 

She left the library through the confines of the bookstacks and the maze of spiral staircases that connected the famous halls. Her body reassured her that this was no bad direction; only after traveling for about five minutes did the bookstore-like smell of leather binders and paper ink begin to transform into the cold, damp smell of the grounds and the sphinxes’ excitement for silhouetted human beings. The sphinxes guarded the back entrance to the school grounds, their rough faces sharp and angles cut, ready to defend Evelyn from any easier sneaks.

Determined not to give up, she continued on. She could still hear her friends whispering about the library’s unbooked books, certain muggle books found in the locked librarian’s office. For a moment, Evelyn felt a sudden hope, but it was cut short as she realized that the library’s specific weight and pH chemistry had been tampered with, leading to significant weight loss. The books were the most valuable and may not be—there were no strings beyond the protective book covers.

“This isn’t personal, it’s business,” she whispered to herself, her voice filled with a mix of spite and disbelief. Her breath was coming out in small, rapid gasps as she approached the rear of the school, where the grounds became a nightmarish maze of closed gates and piles of dead leaves. Her heart was pounding in her chest as she crept through the shadows, every sound from the crickets or branches in the wind making her tense. Her wand was a hunt, an obsession, not just a magic tool.

Finally, she could see the looming silhouette of the main school building, its windows were dim and seemed to change where exactly light was shed and shadowed. She found her way to the main house, the Raven’s Landing. She climbed up to the second floor and into the attic, which had been converted into a dormitory for the eager freshmen.
