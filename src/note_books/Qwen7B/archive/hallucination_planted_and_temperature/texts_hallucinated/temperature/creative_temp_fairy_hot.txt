Long before a dense grove of olives outside a remote hill, a simple yet ambitious miller and his son were preparing a meal. The sky, now alight in a peculiar dance of warm hues that no man or fairy’s candles could mimic, spurred a joy unlike the dance that prompted the crows to align on the branches. Young Cuthbert stood beside his father, his arms lacing to learn of a noble young maiden named Amira, not for her kingdom nor her vast lands, but merely a story of their future spurned from within the layers of whispers.

Farmer of bread and flour were both men; no use of glittering treasures or fair lady's spoilt veils attracted this son so much as to rush their callings to match a sated countenance against a beauty in golden down. Thus Cuthbert approached a storied bridge—the sign leading directly to tales they would follow if not interrupted, built on planks that remained longer than the children who held dreams at one side and logic on the other side.
