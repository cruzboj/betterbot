To comprehend how vaccines work, it is crucial to first understand basic principles of immune function and antigen recognition.

Antigen and Recognition: Vaccination works by exposing the body to an antigen. An antigen can be a protein or fragment of bacteria, a virus, a toxin, or an antibiotic among other components capable of eliting a immune response. These are recognized by immune system proteins called antibodies, which, upon finding an antigen that is recognizable, binds to it and then engages its immune defenses to either disable the harmful agent or remove it altogether, rendering the microbe non-hazardous. Therefore, the effectiveness of this binding between immune cells (mainly T cells and B cells) and the antigen hinges on their being able to specifically recognize one another.
