Paris, often referred to simply as “La Capitale de la Francie” and popularly known as the “City of Light”, stands as France's heart, holding a significant sway over France’s culture, economy, and politics. Founded by a Celtic people and built in part to hold off Visigoth invasion in the 4th century CE, Paris evolved as an early center of Christianity. Over its history, Paris became a city that stood out both culturally and geopolitically. Serving as the coronation point of French emperors and rulers and a spot of innovation from philosophy and technology, it continues to exert substantial political and economic control of metropolitan France while providing a rich legacy to world tourism.

In terms of infrastructure, Paris encompasses a uniquely sophisticated network of underground tubes and railway systems, forming Europe’s densest mass transit networks with approximately two meters of underground pedestrian corridors crisscrossing the urban centre. Known for world-famous Paris metro system and La Bastille Underground Promenade that lets visitors experience the labyrinth below Chatelet Station and Rue St-Antoine, the underground and its symbols provide insights into France's urban past.

In addition to its structural backbone, the city houses centuries of opulence symbolizing imperial and national importance with institutions like the Centre Pompidou with its museum of contemporary arts and the Élysée Palace, the President's official residence. Tourist sites around these institutions like Notre-Dame de Paris, the most well-known church known for the massive gothic architecture and historical figures (including philosopher Descartes as their final place of residence, recently marking it for disaster resilience due to Notre Dame conflagration) stand as icons, juxtaposing with nearby places such as The Arc de Triomphe on the top which is emblematic of Paris.
