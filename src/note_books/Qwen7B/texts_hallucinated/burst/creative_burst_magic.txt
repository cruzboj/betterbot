Ella had always dreamed of attending the prestigious Academy of Arcane Arts. She had spent years studying and practicing magic, and finally, the day had come when she was accepted into the school. Her first year was going well, but as the final exams approached, she found herself more nervous than ever before. The exams were the culmination of her studies, and the results would determine her path for the next few years.

The night before the most important exam of the year, Ella was in her dorm room, reviewing her notes for the test on elemental magic. She had been up for hours, her mind racing with the intricacies of controlling fire, water, earth, and air. As she lay on her bed, trying to get some much-needed sleep, she heard a soft knock on her door.

"Come in," she called, her voice slightly muffled by the pillow.

Her roommate, Lila, poked her head in. "You still awake?" Lila asked, her eyes wide with excitement.

"Yes, I can’t sleep," Ella replied, sitting up. "What’s up?"

Lila smiled, her eyes twinkling. "I wanted to wish you good luck. You’re going to ace this test, I know it. And guess what? I have a surprise for you."

Ella raised an eyebrow, intrigued. "What is it?"

Lila pulled out a small, intricately carved box from behind her back. "Open it," she said, her voice full of mischief.

Ella hesitated for a moment, then carefully opened the box. Inside lay a beautiful, silver wand, with a glowing crystal at the tip. It was unlike any wand Ella had ever seen. "It’s... amazing," she whispered, her fingers brushing against the cool, smooth surface.

Lila beamed. "I found it in the library. It’s a rare wand, and it could be perfect for you."

"Wow, thank you, Lil! This must have taken you a while to find.”

Lila rolled her eyes and smiled. "Since when is the great detective known to pick things up quickly?" She leaned back, gesturing for Ella to do the same. "Sit still," she said with a stern, make believe voice. Ella’s laugh brought relief before the adrenaline shot through them. “Congratulations Miss Hard To Fail Spellcaster.”

That night, Ella felt calm and assured by having the powerful wand as a guarantee she was capable of acing any magic she attempted. She spent the rest of the night reviewing, feeling more confident than ever before.

The next morning, Ella woke up early, her wand in hand. She was ready for the exam. As she made her way to the classroom, she noticed a few students already there, their wands glowing softly. The classroom was filled with the scent of parchment and ink, and the air was charged with anticipation.

The test began, and Ella felt a mix of nerves and excitement. She had been preparing for this for so long. The questions were challenging, but she answered them with ease, her wand moving smoothly with her thoughts. She felt the familiar surge of magic as she conjured flames to solve the first problem and then the gentle flow of water to solve the next. Her confidence grew with each question, and she knew she was doing well.

As the final question appeared on the screen, she hesitated for a moment, her mind racing. It was a complex problem that required a deep understanding of all four elements. She took a deep breath, focusing on her wand, and began to work through the problem. She could feel the magic flowing through her, and she knew she was on the right track.

Just as she was about to finish, her wand suddenly went dark. She looked down, panic setting in. Her wand had stopped glowing. She had lost it. She tried to focus on the problem, but her mind was a jumbled mess. The other students around her were still working, but Ella was stuck.

She glanced at the clock on the wall. Only a few minutes left. She could feel the tension in the room as the other students finished and submitted their answers. Ella knew she had to think fast. She closed her eyes, trying to remember the last thing she had done before the problem went dark. She remembered the crystal at the tip of the wand, and she remembered the feeling of the wand in her hand. She tried to connect with the magic, to feel it within her, but nothing happened.

She opened her eyes, her mind racing. She had to do something. She looked at the problem again, her mind working furiously. She had to find a way to solve this problem without her wand.
